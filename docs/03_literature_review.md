# Literature Review

## Review question and method

This focused review asks: **Which retrieval, matching, personalization, and fairness approaches are suitable for a student-facing internship and entry-level vacancy search MVP when labeled interaction data are not yet available?** The search prioritized job recommender systems, job-resume matching, search and ranking, cold-start, and fairness publications from 2021-2024, with a few current preprints and foundational reviews. The matrix records the method, reported contribution, limitation, and design implication rather than treating model scores as transferable to this project.

The evidence base does not contain a local Kazakhstan benchmark or a user study from this team. Results from Chinese, European, Scandinavian, or industrial datasets cannot be assumed to generalize to Kazakh/Russian listings or to student users.

## Synthesis

Four themes inform the proposal. First, job matching uses both structured fields and free text; explicit attributes such as experience level, location, schedule, salary, and skills should not be discarded [2-5, 10]. Second, interaction-based recommendation relies on history and labels that the new application does not possess, so a content-based baseline is more defensible initially [1, 5, 8-10]. Third, fairness and user trust require explicit attention: deleting a protected field does not guarantee neutral outcomes, and transparent explanations can affect perceived fairness [1, 6, 7, 11-14]. Fourth, industrial retrieval systems use multi-stage architectures, but their scale and data assumptions are inappropriate to copy without evidence [8, 9, 15].

The MVP will therefore use explicit filters and a deterministic, visible fit breakdown (skills, location, experience type, work arrangement). Hard constraints will exclude incompatible records; the remaining optional signals will not be hidden in a single opaque score. Search quality will be evaluated against a human-labeled set, and ranking will remain advisory.
