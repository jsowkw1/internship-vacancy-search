# Relevance Evidence and User Research

## Current evidence status

**No primary user interviews or client agreement were supplied.** The team's existing rationale identifies vacancy discovery as an issue for students and young professionals and notes that postings are spread across services. This is a motivation statement, not a verified finding. No quote, count, or measured search-time claim is reported as fact.

The desk review confirms that the market already includes student or internship features: Enbek describes a practice-placement flow and an early-career category; hh.kz offers internship and no-experience filters; LinkedIn supports internship job type and personalized search; Indeed offers student and internship search guidance. This evidence makes the need for another service a question to validate, not a conclusion.

## Interview plan (3-5 participants)

Recruit students or recent graduates who looked for internships or entry-level work within the previous 12 months. Include a mix of study years and fields where possible. Do not collect phone numbers, CVs, or sensitive demographic data for this coursework study. Obtain consent before taking notes or recording; anonymize any quotations.

### Discussion prompts

1. Tell me about the last time you searched for an internship or your first job. What triggered the search?
2. Which sites, university channels, or personal networks did you use? In what order?
3. What was difficult to find or compare? Please show an example if comfortable.
4. How did you decide whether a vacancy was suitable for a student or a new graduate?
5. Which filters did you use, and which were missing or unclear?
6. How did you track saved opportunities and applications?
7. What information would make you trust or reject a listing?
8. Would a combined catalogue be useful if it showed the original source and last-verified date? Why or why not?
9. What would make a match explanation helpful or misleading?
10. What is one thing this product should not do?

Avoid leading questions such as “Wouldn't one combined platform be easier?” Ask for concrete past behavior before opinions about the proposed solution.

## Synthesis rule

After interviews, group observations by repeated behavior, not by the proposed feature list. Report the denominator (for example, “3 of 5 participants”), include counterexamples, and distinguish direct quotes from the team's interpretation. Do not write results until sessions are completed.
